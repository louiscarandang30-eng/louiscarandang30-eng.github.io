01 — The Problem

What the data looked like before

Raw inventory data from a retail store: product IDs, stock-on-hand quantities, daily sales figures, and supplier lead times. No reorder flags. No risk scoring. No visibility into which products are about to run out.

This is the starting point I've encountered every time — data exists, but it's not telling anyone anything actionable yet. The first job is to build the model that turns raw numbers into early warnings.

My approach: Start with sales velocity — how fast is each SKU actually selling? Then calculate days of supply remaining. Then factor in supplier lead time. The gap between "when you'll run out" and "when the reorder will arrive" is the risk window. Surface any SKU where that gap is negative or dangerously thin.

02 — The Model

Building the risk calculation

Three calculated columns drive everything. Days of supply tells you how long current stock will last at current velocity. Lead time tells you how long it takes to get more. The gap between them is the risk window.

Days of Supply

Stock on hand ÷ average daily sales. Tells you exactly how many days until you run out at current velocity. Simple but the foundation everything else builds on.

Reorder Point

Average daily sales × supplier lead time + safety stock buffer. The minimum stock level that triggers a reorder. If current stock falls below this, you're in the risk window.

Risk Score

Days of supply minus lead time. Negative means you're already behind — you'll stock out before the next order arrives. This is the number that gets flagged.

Safety Stock

Buffer stock holding against demand variance and supplier delays. Calculated as: (max daily sales − avg daily sales) × max lead time. Prevents single-point failures from causing stockouts.

04 — Findings & Recommendations

What the data actually says

This is where the analyst earns their place. Anyone can build a spreadsheet. The value is in translating what the numbers mean into decisions the business needs to make today.

Finding 01 — Immediate Action
P-0019 has 3.9 days of supply against a 21-day lead time. This SKU is already in stockout territory — even if a purchase order is placed today, the product will be unavailable for at least 17 days. Recommend expedited order or sourcing from an alternate supplier. This product needs management attention today.
Finding 02 — Urgent Reorder
P-0017 and P-0055 will stock out within the supplier lead time window. Both products have a negative risk score, meaning current stock will be depleted before a standard reorder arrives. Purchase orders should have been placed already. Priority flag for the purchasing team.
Finding 03 — Reorder Threshold Approaching
P-0088 has 16.7 days of supply against a 7-day lead time. Technically safe for now, but current stock is approaching the reorder point. A purchase order this week keeps the buffer comfortable. Waiting another week risks entering the critical zone if sales accelerate or the supplier has any delay.
Finding 04 — Overstock Flag
P-0064 has 85.5 days of supply — nearly three months of stock. This is capital tied up in inventory that isn't moving. Recommend reviewing whether this SKU has seasonal demand patterns, evaluating a promotional push to accelerate sell-through, or reducing the next purchase quantity to bring inventory turns closer to the category average.
