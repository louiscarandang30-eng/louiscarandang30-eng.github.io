01 — Starting Point

What the raw data looks like

Marketing teams typically export data from each platform separately — Google Ads gives you one CSV, Meta Ads gives you another, email platforms give you a third. Each has different column names, different date formats, and different ways of counting the same thing. The first job is consolidation and validation.

My validation checklist before touching any analysis: Do the date ranges match across platforms? Are conversions being counted the same way (last-click vs view-through)? Does the revenue figure match what the payment processor recorded? Are there any obvious spikes or gaps that suggest a tracking issue? Only once these pass do I move to analysis.

04 — Findings & Recommendations

What this means for budget allocation

Finding 01 — Immediate Win: Email is massively underinvested
Email campaigns are returning 10.5x and 33.5x ROAS at a combined spend of only $1,204. The abandoned cart flow in particular is returning $33 for every $1 spent. This channel has the highest return and the lowest spend. Increasing email volume, frequency, and automation would be the highest-ROI move in the entire mix. Recommend doubling email budget as a first step.
Finding 02 — Stop the Bleeding: Meta Prospecting is destroying value
Meta Prospecting is spending $17,848 to generate $9,380 in revenue — a 0.53x ROAS. This campaign is losing $8,468 in margin each period it runs. Unless there is a strong brand-awareness or audience-building rationale that justifies the loss, this budget should be paused or dramatically restructured. The audience targeting, creative, or offer likely needs a fundamental rethink before any further spend.
Finding 03 — Google Non-Brand needs restructuring
Google Non-Brand is also below break-even at 0.87x ROAS. At $12,807 in spend against $11,136 in revenue, it is losing money on a direct-response basis. This may be acceptable if the keywords are building pipeline for branded searches, but that thesis needs to be tested with attribution analysis. Recommend pausing the weakest ad groups and reallocating spend to Brand Search, which is performing at 4.2x.
Finding 04 — Meta Retargeting has potential but needs improvement
Meta Retargeting at 1.6x ROAS is marginally profitable but well below target. The conversion rate of 7% shows the audience is warm, but the CPA of $21.40 suggests the offer or creative is not closing efficiently. A/B testing the landing page and offer structure for this audience could significantly improve returns given the audience quality already in the funnel.
